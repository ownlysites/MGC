## The one-sentence version

Making Good Choices gives you a complete, personalized credit action plan for free — and then a real person is available to walk through it with you.

---

## The problem it solves

Most people looking at a credit report are looking at a document they cannot read. It is thirty to ninety pages of account codes, dates, grids and abbreviations. They know something on it is costing them — a denied loan, a rate they got stuck with, a deposit they had to leave — but they cannot tell which item is the problem, whether it is even accurate, or what to do about it.

So they do one of three things:

1. **Nothing**, because it is overwhelming.
2. **Pay someone** hundreds of dollars a month to do something they are legally entitled to do themselves for free.
3. **Fire off template letters** they found online, which bureaus and furnishers can dismiss as form letters — burning the one thing that cannot be replaced, which is time.

Making Good Choices exists for the person who wants to handle it themselves but has never been shown how.

## What it actually does

**Step 1 — It asks about your situation.** A short intake: what is going on, whether you have disputed before, whether collectors are contacting you, whether you have been sued, what state you live in, and what you want to happen in the next ninety days.

**Step 2 — It reads your actual credit report.** You upload the PDF. It reads tri-merge reports and single-bureau reports, and you can upload Equifax, Experian and TransUnion separately and it will merge them into one picture. It pulls out your accounts, which bureaus report each one, your late payments and charge-offs, your collections, your hard inquiries with dates, your public records, and your scores.

The whole thing happens inside your browser. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is stored, nothing is sent to a server. Close the tab and it is gone.

**Step 3 — It tells you what it found, in plain language.** Not just a list. It separates what is worth acting on from what is not. It flags inquiries already past the two-year reporting limit that should have fallen off on their own. It tells you how many of your inquiries are actually still affecting your score, because after twelve months they stop counting even though they keep showing. It names which bureaus are reporting on you, so you do not delete something at one bureau and assume it is gone everywhere. If your state has its own credit reporting law, it cites it.

And if your report is clean, it says so, instead of inventing something to dispute.

**Step 4 — It writes your letters.** Bureau disputes, direct disputes to the company that reported the item, method-of-verification demands, permissible purpose demands to whoever pulled your credit without asking, goodwill requests, pay-for-delete offers, and more depending on your situation. Each one carries the right statute, your actual account numbers, and your dates.

Before it writes them, it asks how you want them to sound — hopeful, matter-of-fact, firm, or frustrated — and it asks, in your own words, what this has actually cost you. Your words go into the letter.

**Step 5 — It hands you the whole thing as one document.** The MGC Action Plan: a cover prepared for you by name, what your report shows, everything it found, your plan in phases with the order to work them in, the bureau addresses, every letter on its own page ready to sign and mail, a mailing checklist, and a tracking log to record what you sent and when.

## Why the letters are written the way they are

This is the part most people never hear.

Under federal regulation 12 CFR § 1022.43, a company that reports information about you can refuse to investigate a dispute if it looks like it was **prepared on your behalf by, or submitted on a form supplied by, a credit repair organization**. A letter that reads like a template invites exactly that response.

So the tool does three things about it. It varies the wording so no two people get the same letter. It asks for your voice and your own account of what happened, and puts them in. And it tells you plainly, more than once, to rewrite the letter in your own words before you send it — keeping the statute citations, account numbers and dates exactly as printed, and changing everything around them.

The letters in the packet are also completely clean. No headers, no reference numbers, no notes. Every letter has a separator sheet in front of it, marked "do not mail this page," that carries the guidance. The letter itself is just a letter, because it is going in an envelope to a credit bureau.

## What it costs

Nothing. There is no account, no email required, no subscription, no upsell to unlock the letters.

Every single thing this tool does is something you have the legal right to do yourself at no cost. The tool does not grant you rights — the Fair Credit Reporting Act already did. What it does is show you which of your rights apply to your situation, and hand you the paperwork.

If you do not have a credit report yet, it points you to where to get one, including the free federally mandated option at AnnualCreditReport.com.

## The part that matters most — you are not on your own

Here is what separates this from a template pack on the internet.

A blueprint is only useful if you can read it. And the most expensive mistake in credit repair is not inaction — it is **disputing something that is accurate**, or spending a month chasing an item that was never affecting your score in the first place.

So at two points in the tool — right after it shows you what it found, and again right before you mail anything — it offers you a call.

**Dave Ivery is a certified credit score consultant.** He will go through your report and your plan with you, or connect you with a certified partner if your situation calls for one. Booking is at **ownlyfunds.myfundalytics.com/book**.

Booking a call does not change how any of this works. You still keep your report. You still send your own letters. Everything in the tool stays free to use on your own. The offer is a second set of eyes from someone who reads these reports for a living — not someone taking your file over.

## What Making Good Choices is not

This matters, and the tool says it on the cover of every plan it produces.

- **Not a credit repair organization.** It does not act on your behalf, contact anyone for you, or charge you to do what you can do yourself.
- **Not a law firm.** Nothing in it is legal advice.
- **Not your representative.** Your name goes on the letters. Your signature. Your envelope.
- **Not a promise.** No outcome is guaranteed. Bureaus and furnishers make their own determinations under their own procedures.

It is a self-service tool that hands you a blueprint, and a person who will read it with you if you want that.

## Details worth mentioning

- Current on the rules as of 2026, including the CFPB requirement that you must dispute with the bureau first and either wait 45 days or have your dispute resolved before a complaint will be processed. The tool gates that letter so you do not file early and get it discontinued.
- Knows the fifteen states that restrict medical debt reporting, and says honestly that a federal court has cast doubt on those laws — so you cite yours and expect pushback, rather than assuming it is settled.
- Reminds you every time: certified mail, return receipt requested. The green card is your proof of delivery and it starts the thirty-day clock.
- Tells you when the answer is "leave it alone." A clean report gets told it is clean.
