What makes this different

Built on the laws the industry hides.

The e-OSCAR secret

When you file a dispute online, the credit bureau reduces your entire letter to a three-digit code and two lines of explanation before sending it to the creditor. Your evidence, your legal citations, your arguments — gone. We teach you how to bypass that system.

$100 to $1,000 per violation

The FCRA allows statutory damages of $100–$1,000 per willful violation, plus punitive damages, plus attorney fees. Every letter we generate invokes these penalties. Consumer attorneys work on contingency — the bureau pays your legal fees when you win.

Every letter is one of a kind

Bureaus use AI to flag template letters as "frivolous" and dismiss them without investigation. Our letters use combinatorial variation — tens of thousands of unique permutations per letter type. Your letter has never been sent before.

Nothing leaves your browser

Your credit report contains your full SSN, every account number, every address. Other tools upload all of it to their servers. We process everything locally. When you close this tab, every trace is gone.

Upload both formats if you can — they show different things

Neither report is simply better. They carry different data, and we use whatever we are given.

A tri-merge report shows

  • Where the bureaus contradict each other — one reporting an account as paid as agreed while another reports it late or in collection. That is the hardest kind of dispute to refuse, and a single-bureau pull can never reveal it.
  • Which bureaus carry each account, so you know who to write to.

A single-bureau report shows

  • The month-by-month payment grid — the only place the dates of your delinquencies survive. It is how we work out when each negative item falls off your report, and whether a date has been re-aged.
  • The scoring model's own reason codes, in its order, with its figures.
  • Which accounts are authorized-user accounts, which decides whether an unfamiliar address is worth disputing.

One report still produces a full plan. Two produce a better one. You can add more later — nothing is stored either way.

Full transparency

Disclosures & rights.

What we are (and are not)

Making Good Choices is a free, self-guided educational tool. We are not a credit repair organization, law firm, collection agency, or financial advisor. We do not represent you, file documents on your behalf, or provide legal advice. Every letter we generate is meant for you to review, modify if needed, sign, and send yourself.

You can do all of this yourself — for free

Every right and every process available through this tool is available to you directly at no cost. The credit bureaus, the CFPB, the FTC, and consumer advocacy organizations all provide free information and free dispute processes. We are simply making it easier to navigate and keeping you informed of tactics and timelines the industry tends to obscure.

No guarantees

We do not guarantee any specific outcome. We do not guarantee score increases, account deletions, or any particular response from a credit bureau, creditor, or collector. Results depend entirely on the specifics of your situation, the accuracy of information on your report, and how the other parties respond. Disputing accurate information in bad faith is a violation of federal law.

Affiliate disclosure (FTC 16 CFR Part 255)

We include an affiliate link to SmartCredit, a credit monitoring service. If you sign up through that link, we receive a small commission at no extra cost to you. We disclose this clearly wherever the link appears. You are not required to use that service — the free federally authorized option at AnnualCreditReport.com provides all three credit bureau reports every week at no cost. We recommend starting there.

Privacy

This tool processes everything in your browser. We do not upload your credit report, your identity documents, or any other information to any server. We do not store data between visits. We do not use analytics or trackers. When you close this tab, nothing persists.

Laws referenced

This tool cites and relies on: the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA, 15 U.S.C. §1681 et seq.), the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA, 15 U.S.C. §1692 et seq.), the Credit Repair Organizations Act (CROA, 15 U.S.C. §1679 et seq.), the Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (SCRA, 50 U.S.C. §3901 et seq.), and applicable state consumer protection statutes for CA, TX, FL, NY, IL, PA, OH, GA, NC, and MI. Where we cite a statute, the citation is accurate to the best of our research as of the date the content was written.

When to see an attorney

If you have been sued, are facing wage garnishment, have been personally served court papers, are dealing with complex bankruptcy issues, or are a victim of identity theft requiring a police report, you should consult a licensed attorney. The National Association of Consumer Advocates maintains a directory of attorneys who handle consumer protection cases on contingency — meaning you pay nothing out of pocket and the violating party pays your attorney's fees if you win.

Identity theft warning

Identity theft is a federal crime. Falsely claiming identity theft in order to remove accurate information from a credit report is also a federal crime, punishable by up to five years in prison under 18 U.S.C. §1028. The identity theft affidavit required for a §605B block is signed under penalty of perjury. Proceed with honesty.
